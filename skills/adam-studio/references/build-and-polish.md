# Build, then polish (lessons from the Higgsfield tutorial and its two skills)

Distilled from kaye.creatives' "Claude motion tutorial" on Higgsfield (Oct 2026) and the two skills it ships,
editorial-film-edit and paper-collage-film. What made those edits look finished on the first try was not the prompt:
it was an opinionated visual grammar (a look), real references, hard constraints on the content, and a check of the
result before showing it.

## Two passes, two different jobs

1. Build. Give the look, the references and the constraints, then assemble the whole edit yourself.
   Constraints that never move unless the creator says so: their speech, pauses, clip order, length, format and
   sound. Adapt the imagery and the metaphors to the topic of THIS video.
2. Polish. Keep the approved text and audio untouched; bring every insert into one consistent treatment; tune the
   look's dials (grain down, bloom on, black and white, animated splits); keep the previous version; end with a
   short list of what changed.

In Adam Studio: the guided steps are the build; a second ask on the look step ("polish, keep text and audio") is the
polish. The look's `references/revision-checklist.md` is the polish checklist.

## Check the result itself, not your intentions

- Watch the result in motion and listen to it when you can; contact sheets and numbers help but do not replace it.
  In Adam Studio, `still` on the hook, the boldest moment and the ending at least; say that sound was not checked.
- Fix real defects and recheck only what changed. Never report a check you did not do as passed.

## Code first, generate only what code cannot draw

- Titles, type, collages, paper, transitions, layout: built in the edit (elements and style), not generated.
- Generate only what code cannot make, typically photo cut-outs of objects (a camera, a car, a laptop). No people
  or AI characters unless the creator asks. Generated imagery is never presented as historical evidence.
- Higgsfield (or another generator) only when its MCP is connected in the studio's Connectors AND a budget was
  agreed with the creator: write detailed English prompts (subject, composition, material, light, optics, one
  movement, room left for text). Spending, publishing or messaging third parties never follows from editing alone.

## References

- The creator's references set the vibe: study them (frames for composition, videos for motion and timing), then
  apply their grammar to the creator's own story. Take the technique from the reference, the story from the brand.
- A reference found online teaches; it does not grant the right to reuse it. Pinterest alone does not establish
  usage rights.

## Let the creator choose from what they can see

- Fonts and palettes are chosen from rendered sheets or stills, not from names: show two or three as variants.
- Send something visible early (a beat table, a still of the hook) rather than a long plan.

## Polish rules that recur

- The music (if any) lands on the cuts; deliver a version without music too.
- Nothing covers the hero object, the face or the title.
- No full-screen flashing (photosensitivity).
- Inserts in one consistent treatment; splits only while the presenter is on screen.
- Keep the editable project and the previous export; list the changes.
